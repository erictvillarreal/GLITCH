3  Cerebro 2 — Timeline

Note

Cerebro 2 lives on the cerebro2-dev branch, not yet merged into main. Every commit-hash link in this chapter points into cerebro2-dev specifically — do not expect these paths on main.

3.1 Objective

Survive and generate payouts once an account is funded (post-Combine): an unbounded horizon, no profit target, and success measured as 5 winning days of ≥$150 net rather than a lump sum. See Executive Summary for why this is a fundamentally different optimization problem from Cerebro 1’s.

3.2 Base experiment — the WR/RR decoupling bug

The first grid (scripts/cerebro2_grid_pass1.py, commit 8f7a3a4) swept win rate as a free parameter, independent of RR, over MES and M6E — and its own commit message claimed a no-edge-dependent payout zone at WR≥0.50/RR≥1.5. That claim was retracted explicitly, not silently corrected: WR was decoupled from RR, so the sweep never checked what win rate a zero-edge process would actually produce at each RR (WR_natural = 1/(1+RR), the same gambler’s-ruin relationship underlying Cerebro 1’s G2). Once every row was relabeled against its own WR_natural, the “promising” quadrant of 504 rows turned out to require real edge in 100% of them — an average of +1.01R expected value per trade, a level of edge never found anywhere in this session. Commit 680b66f.

3.3 The exhaustive grid, the Scaling Plan discovery, and the nc_cap bug

A 292,050-run grid (scripts/cerebro2_grid_exhaustive.py) was designed to map the full k×RR×WR×product×account space — motivated by an unverified third-party marketing claim of ~$9,000 average funded-account payout, treated explicitly as a hypothesis to map against, never as a validated number. Commit 48986f9.

While preparing this grid, a deeper problem surfaced: nc_cap (the contract-count ceiling used throughout Cerebro 2’s modeling to that point — 50 for MES, 30 for MGC, etc.) is confirmed by Topstep’s own documentation to be a Combine-phase limit, not the XFA’s. The funded account instead uses a balance-dependent Scaling Plan — contract allowance rises in steps as account balance grows, starting as low as 2–3 lots near $0 balance:

Balance 50K 100K 150K
< $1,500 2 3 3
$1,500–$2,000 3 4 4
$2,000–$3,000 5 5 5
$3,000–$4,500 5 5 10
> $3,000 (100K cap) — 10 —
> $4,500 (150K cap) — — 15

(Lots are mini-equivalents, 10:1 vs. micros, with per-product exceptions for Micro Silver and Micro Bitcoin/Ether.) This meant every prior Cerebro 2 simulation had implicitly assumed a fixed, XFA-lifetime contract count — an optimistic upper bound, not a real result. Commit 23ba2e8. The exhaustive grid was allowed to finish under the old fixed-nc assumption and kept only as a labeled upper-bound reference, never as a final result.

Follow-up validation (Tarea 1) found this did not change the answer for the winning region specifically: simulate_xfa_lifetime_dynamic_nc() was built with nc_today = min(nc_designed, nc_legal_del_día) — a first version that used the legal maximum unconditionally was caught and fixed before being reported (see Methodology & Discipline for why this class of bug matters). For the actual top-5 candidates (all 150K, k=2, designed nc between 3 and 6), the Scaling Plan’s legal floor at $0 balance (30 micro-contracts for a 150K account) is already far above the designed nc — so min() always resolves to the smaller designed value, and fixed-nc and dynamic-nc give identical results for these candidates specifically (confirmed bit-exact at n_paths=10,000). This does not generalize to candidates with larger designed nc or smaller accounts, which were not tested.

3.4 Incident: vectorization bug turns a 2-hour grid into a projected 70 hours

The exhaustive grid was launched after an approved preflight benchmark. At 44 minutes and only 1% complete, the script’s own ETA had climbed to ~4,200 minutes (~70 hours) — the run was stopped before burning unsupervised compute. Root cause: simulate_xfa_lifetime() had never been vectorized — a scalar per-path Python loop whose runtime depended entirely on how many days each path survived before blowing, varying ~75x across the grid (12ms for a fast-blowing low-RR case vs. 868ms for a slow, high-survival high-RR corner) — and the preflight’s single benchmark point happened to sample the fast extreme.

Fixed by rewriting simulate_xfa_lifetime() to vectorize over the path axis with NumPy array operations, validated bit-exact against the old scalar implementation across 32 cases (both MLL policies, all 3 account sizes, low/high WR/RR, same seed) before replacing it — full 134/134 test suite unchanged. Post-fix variance dropped to ~2.3x, and the real grid runtime came in at 51 minutes against the original ~70-hour projection. Commit 4842fea.

3.5 Pivot to a real-edge search

The user redirected Cerebro 2 from “modest pure geometry” toward a genuine statistical-edge search, with success criteria distinct from Cerebro 1’s: no speed requirement, but N>200, p<0.05, and reproducible — explicitly invoking the two historical p=0.165/p=0.081 candidates from Cerebro 1 that never reproduced fresh, as the standard any new candidate must clear.

3.5.1 712 tests, eight candidates that pass p<0.05, zero that clear the bar

Across an individual-product grid (96 tests), a 15-pair cross-product grid (600 tests, including all 14 previously-untested pairs), and a dedicated ZC grid (16 tests) — 712 tests total — eight candidates passed the nominal p<0.05 threshold:

Candidate N p Note
M2K–M6E weekly hold=3d/fade 128 0.0003 Strongest of the search; shares the M6E leg with several other hits
Spread MES–M2K daily hold=5d/momentum 86 0.0019 Confirmed by 2 independent constructions
Spread MES–M2K daily hold=10d/fade 47 0.022 Confirmed by 2 constructions
MES+MGC weekly hold=5d/fade (pooled) 170 0.048 —
MES+MGC weekly/fade, ex-Friday 135 0.033 In-sample refinement — more skepticism warranted, not less
MCL daily hold=2d/fade 168 0.028 From a 96-test grid (~4.8 false positives expected by chance)
M6E daily hold=3d/fade 126 0.032 From the same 96-test grid
ZC daily hold=2d/fade 125 0.023 All 7 fade configs positive EV, all 7 momentum configs negative — internally consistent

None reach N>200. None are used in production. The eight hits also share legs (MES appears in 6 of the 15 strongest pair rows; M6E in several others), so they cannot be treated as independent confirmations of a common effect. A candidate “Friday effect” appeared in three of these results but flipped sign or vanished under closer inspection (MCL’s best day is Friday, not worst; the MES–M2K hold=10d spread’s Friday behavior is the opposite of its hold=5d sibling) — treated as fragile, not structural. A dedicated barrier-geometry sweep on the two strongest candidates confirmed the inherited 2.5/1.5 PT/SL ratio was already near-optimal for this range — the geometry was not accidentally handicapping the results. Sources: GLITCH_RESEARCH_LOG.md, “Cierre de gaps”, commit 366f5f8, commit 6a606d7.

3.6 The central structural finding: Combine and XFA are different optimization problems

With no real-edge candidate clearing N>200, attention returned to the geometry-pure MGC/150K candidate (SL=TP=364 ticks, WR=0.5, k=2, nc=6) — designed to survive the XFA phase (46.2% probability of at least one lifetime payout, $2,169 expected value, run through the already-validated simulate_xfa_lifetime_dynamic_nc). This candidate had never been run through the Combine phase it must first pass.

Result: a 46.9% Combine pass rate (200,000-path Monte Carlo via the same TopstepMonteCarloSimulator engine already used and audited for G2) — roughly half of G2’s 81.4%. This directly confirmed the user’s suspicion that a geometry designed to tolerate few consecutive losses once funded (low nc, wide symmetric barriers) is not the same geometry that clears the Combine’s bounded profit-target race quickly. Commit 1cff838; see also the retrospective framing in GLITCH_RESEARCH_LOG.md, “HALLAZGO ESTRUCTURAL CENTRAL DE CEREBRO 2.” No future Cerebro 2 candidate’s Combine pass rate should be assumed from another candidate’s number — it must be measured directly, every time.

3.7 Two-stage cash-flow model and empirical validation of the MGC/150K candidate

A full two-stage Monte Carlo (Combine pool of 200,000 outcomes + XFA pool of 50,000 episodes, sampled with replacement across 20,000 independent 365-day trajectories, paying real Combine/activation fees each attempt) produced a distribution, not a point estimate:

Metric p10 p25 Median p75 p90
1-year cumulative payout $15,809 $22,298 $31,257 $42,076 $53,861
Max capital cushion needed $298 $447 $894 $1,639 $2,679

Tail risk was quantified, not just described: probability that any single attempt reaches at least one payout is 46.9% × 46.4% = 21.8%; probability of needing ≥5 consecutive failed attempts before a payout is 37.5%; ≥10 consecutive failures, 11.0%. The $2,679 (p90) cushion figure reflects roughly 18 paid Combine attempts absorbed before enough payout arrives to compensate. Commit 1cff838.

The candidate’s two core assumptions were then validated against real MGC price data rather than left as Monte Carlo assumptions:

  • Empirical win rate: first measurement gave an alarming 34.85% vs. the theoretical 50% — traced to the same time-exit dilution bug already found and fixed twice earlier in this session (Methodology & Discipline) — 30.2% of trades expire by time rather than TP/SL for this wide-barrier geometry. The corrected conditional win rate (TP/(TP+SL), excluding time-exits): 49.97%, essentially exact against the 50% theoretical assumption. Commit d1f11fa.
  • Sub-period stability: confirmed stable across 3 independent calendar sub-periods (49.90%, 49.92%, 49.89% — all within 0.15pp of 50%). Commit 3f0bd07.
  • Long/short asymmetry: not persistent — favors long in sub-periods 1–2 (+9.98pp, +15.23pp) but flips to favor short in sub-period 3 (−3.64pp). This is evidence for, not against, the already-chosen alternating design: since the asymmetry is noise rather than a stable bias, alternating protects against betting on either side.
  • Combine pass-rate sensitivity: real but contained. A ±5–8pp WR shift near this RR=1.0 equilibrium point moves the Combine pass rate by ~13–20pp (range observed: 30.2%–63.7% across long-only/short-only/sub-period scenarios) — a structural fragility of operating this close to the WR=50%/RR=1.0 break-even point, flagged as a model risk for the user’s production decision, not a defect requiring a design change.

3.8 Status

The MGC/150K candidate is validated on both of its core assumptions (Combine pass rate, empirical WR) as of 07 September 2026. A third scheduler (scheduler/geometry_mgc_scheduler.py) has been built for paper trading on Railway — its construction, the RTH-window recalibration finding made along the way, and remaining infrastructure blockers are covered in Infrastructure & Operations.
